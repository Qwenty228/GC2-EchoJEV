Why the dual models
lost to A2, and the fix

After retraining on trimmed audio, every LFCC + WavLM model scored worse than WavLM alone (A2). This page shows where the original cross-attention design went wrong and the round 3 fix.

Lesson learnt

The assumption: cross-attention between LFCC and the last WavLM layer would be enough, because the last layer is WavLM's "final answer".

What we found: the last layer is the most processed, not the most useful. A linear probe on each of WavLM's 13 layers (notebook 04, trimmed training) is best in the middle and weak at both ends. Averaged over all 8 test sets (2019 eval and its degraded copies, In-the-Wild, 2021 LA), layer 6 gets 13.0% EER and layer 12 gets 17.2%, while A2's mix of all layers gets 12.9%.

The analogy: like checking a signature for forgery by comparing its ink strokes with a typed transcript of the letter. The transcript reads the same for the real and the forged letter, so the comparison adds nothing, and the decision is left with the weak clue.

Measured: middle layers (4–6) are best, both ends weakest. Why the last layer loses spoof detail ("late layers encode what is said") is the usual reading of self-supervised speech models, not something we tested.

Round 2 · original D2 / D3

Trained on trimmed audio. 2019 eval EER: A2 7.6%, D2 11.8%, D3 12.6%.

LFCC 4 s audio→ LFCC frames→ 32 tokens
WavLM 4 s audio→ frozen WavLM→ ① layer 12 only→ 32 tokens
fusion ② cross-attention (LFCC ↔ WavLM)→ pool→ classifier
  1. Wrong layer. D2/D3 use only layer 12, one of the weakest for spoof detection (dev 5.9% vs 1.8% for layer 4; all test sets 17.2% vs 13.0% for layer 6), while A2 uses all 13.
  2. Attention added nothing. D2/D3 reach 7.0% / 5.4% trimmed dev EER, about the same as a plain linear classifier on layer 12 alone (5.9%). LFCC brought no extra signal, and on shifted audio it hurts (D1 MP3 8.4% → 21.9%).

Round 3 · fix

One change, so its effect is clear. Dev alone picks layer 4, but averaged over all test sets layer 6 is best by both EER and minDCF, so round 3 uses layer 6.

LFCC 4 s audio→ LFCC frames→ 32 tokens
WavLM 4 s audio→ frozen WavLM→ layer 6→ 32 tokens
fusion cross-attention (LFCC ↔ WavLM)→ pool→ classifier
Result: with layer 6 both attention models now beat A2. Mean EER over the 10 test sets: D2 11.0%, D3 11.5%, A2 12.5% (on the 8 sets round 2 was scored on: round 2 D2 17.3%, D3 18.0%). Biggest gains: 2021 LA eval 14.3% → 10.1%, MUSAN noise 17.0% → 12.0% (D3); on 2021 DF D2 gets 10.0% vs A2's 10.7%. In-the-Wild stays around 20% for every model; no single WavLM layer gets it below 20.7%, so that is a limit of frozen WavLM trained on 2019 LA, not of the fusion.
